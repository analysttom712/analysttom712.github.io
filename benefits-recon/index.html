Monthly benefits premium reconciliation pack

A sample of the reporting I build for benefits operations: voluntary payroll deductions matched to carrier billing each pay period, a retiree medical and dental premium subledger that ties to the general ledger, continuation coverage receivables with the 2% administrative fee, and an exception list that traces every variance down to one participant.

Invented data, fictional groups. Every participant, district, plan, person number and dollar figure on this page is made up by a seeded generator that runs in your browser. Nothing here comes from any employer, carrier, plan or person, and no employer is named or described. Reporting month is October 2026; January through October are closed.

The data model behind it

A star schema. Every relationship is one-to-many in a single direction, from the dimension to the fact, and every fact joins on the person number. Keeping the direction single means a filter on a district or a plan reaches the facts and stops there, instead of travelling back up another table and widening the result.

The eight exception rules are measures over these tables, not separate extracts. The activity log is what lets a variance be explained rather than only counted: it carries the date an event was keyed, which is usually later than the date it happened, and that gap is the cause of most of what lands on the exception list.

The pack

Each section below is closed until you open it. The headline on every one is live: change the month, the district or the period above and every summary updates, whether the section is open or not.

What the close found

    Summary tie-out: billed, collected, posted

    Three strands in one view. Billed is what the subledger invoiced, collected is cash applied to those invoices, and posted is what reached the general ledger. The two reconciling columns are the only things allowed to sit between the subledger and the GL, and both are itemized below.

    Receipts in transit are collections taken on the last business day of the month that post to the GL in the following month. Unapplied cash is money received that exceeds the invoice it arrived against and sits in suspense until it is applied or refunded.

    Retiree premium subledger

    The centerpiece. Retiree medical and dental premium is billed from the subledger, drafted or paid by the retiree, and posted to the GL. Each plan line shows what the eligibility record said the premium should be, what was actually billed, and what came in.

    Expected premium is the plan rate for the participant's tier on the bill date, taken from the plan table and the eligibility record. Any line where billed does not equal expected is picked up by the expected-versus-actual rule and traced to the participant.

    Voluntary deductions versus carrier billing

    Each pay period, deductions taken in payroll system reporting are matched to the carrier's invoice, plan by plan and then participant by participant. A deduction with no invoice line, an invoice line with no deduction, and a deduction that does not equal the invoiced rate are three different problems with three different owners.

    This is why the variance is reported gross and not only net. The carrier overbills some participants and underbills others in the same cycle, and the two directions partly cancel. The net is what lands in the benefits clearing account; the gross is what somebody has to go and correct, participant by participant, and they are never the same number. A clearing balance that does not return to zero within two cycles is escalated to the group's account manager with the participant list attached.

    Continuation coverage billing and receivables

    Continuation participants are billed the full group premium plus a 2% administrative fee and pay by personal remittance, so this is the strand where people stop paying, pay part of the bill, or pay late. The grace period runs 30 days; a participant who passes it is flagged for a termination notice.

    Outstanding balance aging

    Open invoice items at the reporting month end, aged from the bill date. Aging is calculated at the invoice-item level, not on a participant's net balance, so a participant who pays this month's bill while an older one is still open still shows in the older bucket.

    Exception list

    Eight rules run against the facts every time the pack is built. Each one is a measure over the same star schema, so a count on this page and a row in the evidence table below are the same calculation. Pick a rule to see the participants behind the count.

    How the checks work

    Completeness first, rates rebuilt rather than trusted, cash matched item by item, and only two reconciling items allowed
    • Completeness before anything else. Every participant with an active coverage record in a month must have exactly one invoice item for each elected plan. Counts are compared both ways: invoice items without an eligibility record, and eligibility records without an invoice item.
    • Expected premium is rebuilt, not trusted. The rate is recalculated from the plan table and the participant's tier on the bill date, including mid-year rate changes, and compared to what was billed. Rate changes and tier changes are the two places where this breaks.
    • Cash is matched at the item level. A payment is applied against a specific invoice item, so a participant can be current on this month and 90 days late on an older one at the same time. Partial payments stay partial; they are not netted into a single balance.
    • The subledger ties to the GL every month, with a named reconciling item or none at all. Receipts in transit and unapplied cash are the only two permitted, and each is backed by the list of items that make it up. An unexplained difference stops the pack.
    • Dates drive the rules. Age-26 and age-65 are evaluated against the bill date, not the run date. Death-related rules compare the event date with the bill date and the draft date separately, because a bill raised after a death and a draft taken after a death are two different conversations and two different fixes.
    • Every count is clickable down to one person number. A number on a summary that cannot be opened to the rows behind it is not finished.
    • Tools: Power BI with Power Query and DAX over payroll system reporting, carrier billing files and the general ledger; Python and SQL for the file intake and matching. Claude (AI) wrote the Python, SQL and DAX at my direction; I set the vendor formats, matching rules and checks, and reviewed every result.
